## 1. What was implemented

Both models live in the **C family = `CHAOSCache`** (the L1D injector), and
both hook the **MSHR resource accounting** of the mounted L1D. The blocker
"C14/C15: event source only" meant the *observation* existed (the H4
`mshr_alloc/merge/fill/free` events shipped in R4-3) while the *injection*
had no hook: no repo hook sat on the MSHR **release** point (C14) or on the
**capacity-arbitration** point (C15). Those two points are `BaseCache`'s:

* `recvTimingResp` → `mshrQueue.deallocate(mshr)` (the serviced entry
  returns to the free list) — **C14**'s release point;
* `allocateMissBuffer` → `mshrQueue.isFull()` (the new miss allocation runs
  the capacity arbitration) — **C15**'s arbitration point.

The C11 merge hook (`handleTimingReqMiss`, coalescing arm) and the C12 fill
hook (`recvTimingResp` top, before `popSenderState`) are both elsewhere, so
neither could express these faults. That is the honest reason the two models
were blocked, and it is now removed.

### 1.1 C14-a — MSHR busy/occupied stuck-at-1 (resource leak)

**Injection.** `targetField="mshr_busy_leak"`. `BaseCache::recvTimingResp`
consults the new `chaosMshrBusyHook` IMMEDIATELY BEFORE
`mshrQueue.deallocate(mshr)`; when the injector consents, the caller **skips
the deallocation** (and the `clearBlocked(Blocked_NoMSHRs)` bookkeeping that
belongs to it). The serviced entry therefore stays in the MSHR queue's
allocated list and **never returns to the free list** — the busy/occupied
state is stuck at 1.

* The corresponding miss **completed normally**: `serviceMSHRTargets` already
  delivered the fill before this point (the 03 sheet's "对应 miss 正常完成"),
  so the consumer got its data; only the **slot** is lost.
* Once the leaked slot is the last free-demand entry, `mshrQueue.isFull()`
  stays true, the CPU side stays blocked on `Blocked_NoMSHRs` and prefetch is
  starved → the 03 sheet's "容量退化和Timeout".
* **One entry per run** (the 03 sheet's "每次运行只固定一个表项"): a
  single-slot member (`mshr_busy_victim`) plus the campaign's `maxFaults=1`.

**Trigger discipline.** Event-normalized `ChaOSLsuTrigger` on the
release-event stream: `onAttempt()` once per hook invocation, then F6 →
`onF6Event(false)` (the release hook IS the attempt stream; no double count —
R4-5.1-review-I1), everything else → `onEligible()`. The NINTH F6 event
source (MSHR completing-and-should-release) is registered in
`chaos_lsu_trigger.hh`.

**Observation (new kind `mshr_busy`, layer L2).** `mshr_id`, `entry_index`
(= the slot identity), `cache_line`, `field=busy`,
`state_before=should_release` → `state_after=stuck_busy`. The `inj` node is
`corr=origin` with `before`/`after` = the **occupancy pair** (the occupancy
the correct release would have left vs. the occupancy with the release
skipped) — a 4th per-model faulted-value type after C12's tick pair, L04's
seq pair and P06's classification pair. `registerMshrBusyFault` also puts the
**whole leaked line** in the faulted-byte registry so later events on it join
address-only (`corr=addr_fallback`, the labeled C-family arm — no seq primary
at this site). Teardown line `CHAOS_MSHR_BUSY: … leaks= slot_held=
released_normally=`.

### 1.2 C15-b — MSHR capacity-arbitration "fake free"

**Injection.** `targetField="mshr_occupancy"`. `BaseCache::allocateMissBuffer`
(inline in `base.hh`) calls the new out-of-line helper
`BaseCache::chaosMshrOccArbitrate(pkt, mshr)` at its TAIL — right after the
new miss allocation ran the capacity arbitration. When the queue is FULL
(`mshrQueue.isFull()` — the only public capacity predicate; the private
`allocated` counter is unreachable, so the 03 sheet's "接近或达到容量上限"
is realised as "达到" honestly) and the trigger consents, the injector
**fabricates a free slot**: the helper calls
`clearBlocked(Blocked_NoMSHRs)`, the CPU side retries, and **exactly one**
further miss allocation consumes a *reserve* entry BEYOND the arbitration's
capacity. The arbitration's allow/deny decision is genuinely wrong — the 03
sheet's "仅在仲裁结果真正允许…一次 miss 分配时计为 activated".

**Read-back (the real `activated`).** `noteMshrAllocAttempt` is called at the
TOP of every subsequent `allocateMissBuffer` on the mounted cache. A pending
fabricated slot is CONSUMED iff that allocation still runs while the queue is
full — that allocation is the over-capacity one: the fault is then REGISTERED
(`registerMshrArbFault`) and `mshr_over_commit` (the honest activation
counter) is bumped. If the queue drained in between, the flag is dropped and
**nothing is recorded** — an honest Inject-not-activated sample (the fire is
still visible in the injection log and the trigger funnel). Teardown line
`CHAOS_MSHR_OCC: … fires= activated= occ_at_fire=`.

**Observation (new kind `mshr_arb`, layer L2).** `mshr_id`, `entry_index`
(= the MSHR occupancy at the over-commit — the model's faulted bookkeeping),
`cache_line`, `field=capacity`, `state_before=full_blocked` →
`state_after=over_commit_alloc`. `inj` is `corr=origin` with `before`/`after`
= the occupancy pair. The over-committed request's whole line goes into the
faulted-byte registry (`corr=addr_fallback`).

**L0 record.** The **fire** writes a `Field: mshr_occupancy (C15-b fake-free
at capacity)` line (the C-family fire-side record, like C11/C12 — the
campaign's `activated` proxy), and the **materialization** additionally writes
`Field: mshr_occupancy_materialized (…)`. The honest activation count is the
teardown `CHAOS_MSHR_OCC activated=`.

## 7. Unresolved / residual risks

1. **Compile-unverified C++** (§6) — fix-ups possible at build time.
2. **C14 same-line re-merge consequence (documented, not fixed).** Skipping
   the deallocation leaves the leaked MSHR in the queue's allocated list, so
   a *later* miss to the **same line** can still `findMatch` it and merge
   into the stuck entry — that request then waits for a response that will
   never come. That is a genuine hard consequence and it is exactly what the
   03 sheet asks to record ("是否形成死锁"); it is asserted by the model's
   own wording ("状态卡死会逐步耗尽可用 MSHR"), but it means the observable
   may be a per-line hang as well as pure capacity degradation. The pilot
   must record BOTH (the teardown `CHAOS_MSHR_BUSY slot_held=` plus the
   oracle's Timeout). A "poison the leaked entry's match address" variant was
   considered and rejected: `QueueEntry::blkAddr` is not publicly writable,
   and silently removing the hazard would hide a real modelled effect.
3. **C15 W11 cells stay LICENSE-blocked** (not part of this landing's
   scope): `WL_BLOCKED["SPEC"]` is evaluated before `MODEL_BLOCKED`, so
   deleting the C15 block entry does not unlock `C15-F{0,4,6}-W11`. Recorded
   so no one mistakes them for a missed unlock.
4. **C15 fire/materialization race window.** The fabricated slot is consumed
   by the *next* `allocateMissBuffer` on the mounted cache. If that
   allocation never comes within the remaining run (the simulation ends, or
   the CPU side had nothing left to send), the fire is an honest
   Inject-not-activated: the injection log shows the fire, the prop stream
   has no chain, and the teardown's `fires > activated` records it. Do not
   read a `fires=1 activated=0` sample as a defect.
5. **C15 `entry_index` semantics.** For `mshr_arb` the required `entry_index`
